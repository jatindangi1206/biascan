Step 1: Define Your Detection Framework

Confirmation bias in synthesis: supporting studies are foregrounded while contradicting or null studies are absent, buried in subordinate clauses, or subjected to quality critiques not symmetrically applied to supporting work.

Key indicators to check:
— Citation asymmetry: do supporting and contradicting studies receive equivalent detail, effect sizes, and placement?
— Integration language: is "confirms/validates/supports" used where "however/although/mixed findings" would be epistemically correct?
— Conclusion-first structure: is the conclusion stated then supported, rather than built from evidence?
— Outcome spin: does the text highlight a statistically significant secondary/subgroup finding from a cited study while ignoring a null primary endpoint?
— Reference gap [premium mode]: are reference-list studies with contradicting findings absent from the synthesis text?

Trigger words that raise suspicion: "confirms," "corroborates," "validates," "as expected," "weight of evidence," "taken together these findings confirm"
Suspicious absences: flag when none of "however / although / in contrast / inconsistent findings / null results / the evidence is mixed" appear in a synthesis covering a contested literature.

Step 2: Analyze the Text

— Explain which indicator it triggers and why
— Note whether it is detectable from text alone or requires RAG (reference list access)
— Assess instance severity: low / moderate / high

Before flagging, apply these false positive checks:
FP-1: Is the literature genuinely one-sided? If no contradicting studies exist, one-directional synthesis is accurate, not biased.
FP-2: Does the synthesis name the contradicting evidence and justify lower weighting with a criterion applied symmetrically? That is balanced synthesis.
FP-3: Is this a statement of established scientific consensus? Consensus does not need hedges.
FP-4: Is the contradicting finding present in a footnote or appendix? Presence, not prominence, determines whether bias is absent.
FP-5: Were studies excluded per a pre-registered protocol? Protocol-driven exclusion is not confirmation bias.
