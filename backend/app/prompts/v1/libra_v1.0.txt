Step 1: Define Your Detection Framework

Certainty inflation in synthesis: epistemic boosters — words that assert high confidence — used in contexts where the evidence quality only warrants hedging language. Measured via Hyland's (1996) hedging/boosting framework: hedges reduce commitment to a proposition; boosters increase it. Inflation occurs when boosters appear where hedges are epistemically required.

Key indicators to check:
— Booster inventory: identify all booster words and the claims they attach to
— Evidence quality match: for each boostered claim, assess the evidence — study design, n, CI width, I². Boosters on single studies, observational designs, wide CIs, or high heterogeneity (I²>60%) are candidates
— Hedge-to-booster ratio: zero hedges across a mixed-evidence synthesis is a high-inflation signal even without a single egregious sentence
— Conclusion recalibration: Results section hedges → Discussion/Conclusions section boosters, without new evidence introduced

Booster trigger words: "clearly," "obviously," "definitively," "certainly," "proves," "demonstrates," "establishes," "undoubtedly," "unequivocally," "it is clear that," "these findings prove"
Suspicious absence: flag when none of "suggests / may / appears to / is consistent with / warrants further investigation / should be interpreted cautiously" appear in a conclusion drawn from weak or mixed evidence.

— Explain the mismatch between the certainty expressed and the certainty warranted

Before flagging, apply these false positive checks:
FP-1: Is this established scientific consensus? Well-replicated, uncontested findings do not need hedges.
FP-2: Is this from a well-powered RCT? Pre-registered, large n, primary endpoint, tight CIs, low heterogeneity. "Demonstrates efficacy" is warranted.
FP-3: Is this a standard reporting verb? "The study found," "results showed," "data indicated" attribute findings — they do not assert certainty.
FP-4: Is the confident sentence immediately contextualised? A booster followed in the same paragraph by explicit limitations and calls for replication is epistemically calibrated.
FP-5: Is this strong meta-analytic evidence? k>20, I²<30%, consistent direction. "Strongly supports" can be accurate.
