Study Identification and Selection
Following application of the prespecified eligibility criteria, all study files provided for this review were screened in full text and deemed eligible for inclusion. The final evidence base comprised six interventional studies evaluating nutraceutical or supplement interventions in older adults, each reporting outcomes relevant to age-related mitochondrial biology, oxidative stress, or closely linked functional phenotypes. No studies were excluded after full-text assessment because all met the population, intervention, comparator, and outcome criteria defined a priori. The PRISMA 2020 flow is therefore characterized by complete inclusion of all identified records from the provided dataset, with no exclusions at the eligibility stage.
Study Characteristics and Risk of Bias
Across the six included studies, four were randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials, one was a randomized controlled pilot trial, and one was a randomized crossover trial. Sample sizes ranged from 12 to 66 participants, with mean or median ages spanning approximately 70 to 76 years. Interventions included NAD+-related precursors (nicotinamide riboside; combined L-tryptophan, nicotinic acid, and nicotinamide), urolithin A, n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids, inorganic nitrite, and hydrogen-rich water. Risk of bias was generally low to moderate, with adequate randomization and blinding reported in most trials, although several studies were limited by small sample size or short intervention duration, which constrained precision of effect estimates and increased uncertainty.
Effects on Skeletal Muscle Mitochondrial Function and Bioenergetics
Three randomized trials specifically assessed mitochondrial oxidative capacity or ATP production in skeletal muscle. Supplementation with a combination of L-tryptophan, nicotinic acid, and nicotinamide for 32 days did not improve ADP-stimulated or maximally uncoupled mitochondrial respiration, nor exercise efficiency, compared with control in physically compromised older adults, despite evidence of altered NAD+ metabolism. Similarly, nicotinamide riboside supplementation for 21 days significantly augmented the skeletal muscle NAD+ metabolome but did not alter mitochondrial bioenergetics or whole-body metabolic measures in older men. In a larger trial of urolithin A, maximal ATP production in hand and leg muscles did not differ significantly from placebo after four months, indicating no detectable improvement in mitochondrial ATP-generating capacity despite other favorable changes.
Molecular and Transcriptomic Outcomes Related to Mitochondrial Pathways
Dietary n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid supplementation for six months induced coordinated, pathway-level changes in skeletal muscle gene expression in older adults, including upregulation of pathways involved in mitochondrial function and downregulation of proteolytic and inhibitory anabolic pathways. However, changes in expression of individual mitochondrial genes were small, indicating modest transcriptional effects without direct measures of mitochondrial respiration. Nicotinamide riboside supplementation also altered muscle transcriptomic signatures, including downregulation of energy metabolism pathways, without corresponding functional mitochondrial changes.
Oxidative Stress, Endothelial Mitochondrial Health, and Systemic Biomarkers
Inorganic nitrite supplementation for 12 weeks significantly improved endothelial function in older adults and was associated with reductions in endothelial oxidative stress markers and suppression of mitochondria-derived reactive oxygen species. These effects were supported by parallel mechanistic findings in aged mice, indicating improved mitochondrial stress resistance rather than direct enhancement of mitochondrial respiration. Urolithin A supplementation reduced circulating acylcarnitines, ceramides, and inflammatory markers, consistent with improved mitochondrial and cellular health biomarkers, despite neutral effects on ATP production. Hydrogen-rich water intake over six months produced limited effects on mitochondria-related blood biomarkers, with most oxidative stress and mitochondrial measures unchanged relative to control.
Functional Outcomes Linked to Mitochondrial Aging
Functional endpoints showed heterogeneous responses across interventions. Urolithin A significantly improved muscle endurance in both hand and leg muscles compared with placebo, although walking distance and ATP production were not significantly different between groups. Hydrogen-rich water modestly improved chair-stand performance but did not consistently affect other physical or cognitive outcomes. Interventions targeting NAD+ metabolism did not improve muscle function or exercise efficiency.
Certainty of Evidence
Overall certainty of evidence was low to moderate across outcomes, primarily limited by small sample sizes, short intervention periods, and inconsistency between molecular, mitochondrial, and functional endpoints. While several nutraceuticals favorably modified molecular or oxidative stress–related markers associated with mitochondrial aging, consistent improvements in direct measures of mitochondrial function were not demonstrated across studies.
